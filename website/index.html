Road trauma continues beyond the crash

Fatalities receive attention, but many more people live through road crashes and enter hospital. This story follows that burden through time, road use, age, sex, place and First Nations status.

Explore the evidence

Counts are not risk rates

These charts show the number of hospitalisations, not rates per person or per trip. Places and groups with more people or more travel may record more hospitalisations even when each person’s risk is lower.

The national picture

Hospitalisations rose over the decade

Annual totals increased from 34,033 in 2011 to 39,505 in 2021. The fall in 2020 interrupted the broader upward pattern, before counts returned close to the earlier peak.

06

Methods and limitations

What these charts can and cannot show

Source

Bureau of Infrastructure and Transport Research Economics (BITRE), Hospitalised Injury Data, covering 2011-2021.

Processing

KNIME cleaned category labels, checked duplicates, created dates and ordering fields, aggregated selected views and exported the chart-ready CSV.

Missing values

Published values marked “n.p.” remain missing. They are excluded from sums and are never converted to zero.

Interpretation

Counts do not measure individual risk. Comparisons across places and groups are affected by population size and exposure.
